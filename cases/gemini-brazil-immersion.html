Context

This was a large-scale, multi-phase study to give the Google Gemini executive team a deep immersion into the lives of its users in Brazil.

The goal was to build a rich, contextual understanding of how Brazilian students and micro-entrepreneurs use multimodal AI inputs (text, voice and camera). We also sought to identify the personality traits and cultural characteristics AI should reflect to be better accepted and more useful in the Brazilian market.

All of this in a local landscape where WhatsApp and voice messages are everywhere and many users have limited data plans (“data poverty”).

Objectives

The study sought quantitative and qualitative insights, both remote and in person, on three topics:

Multimodal use

  • Analyze how Brazilian students and micro-entrepreneurs use and switch between voice, camera and text inputs.
  • Identify specific use cases and how each interaction mode aligns with Brazilian culture.

Model behavior and personality (AI)

  • Discover the persona, tone, interaction style and cultural values the AI model should reflect.
  • Evaluate how Gemini’s personality compares with other LLMs users prefer.
  • Understand AI safety concerns such as truthfulness, skill loss and loss of control.
  • Determine preferences between a formal and informal tone, and between longer and shorter answers.

App response quality and UI

  • Understand how local UI paradigms (such as WhatsApp) shape preferences for visual density and conversational guidance.
  • Define what users consider a “useful visual summary” versus “visual clutter”.
  • Identify which visual metaphors (colors, icons, emojis) signal trust versus spam or advertising in Brazil.

Methodology

The study had several phases and, during the immersion, mixed methods on parallel tracks, with 5 researchers allocated in total.

  1. Ethnographies (90 min): 10 sessions, split between 2 researchers. I moderated half of them.
  2. In-depth interviews in a mirror room (90 min): 10 in-lab sessions at the Google office, split between 2 other researchers.
  3. Panel, run by the Ipsos team.
  4. Executive Learning Dinner with the executive team, run by the Ipsos team.
  5. Co-creation workshop, run by the Ipsos team.
  6. Closing workshop, run by the Ipsos team.

Our role

At Ipsos, we handled recruitment, logistics and moderation, and delivered cultural and design recommendations.

I led the entire operation, with more than 20 people across moderators, designers and logistics.
