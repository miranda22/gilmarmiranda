Context

This was a qualitative UX study to evaluate two strategic initiatives within the WhatsApp ecosystem in Brazil: Biz AI and Pix Native. The study ran in a production environment (Prod/GK), so participants interacted with real experiences using their own phones, their own bank accounts and real Pix transactions.

Today, when consumers buy from businesses on WhatsApp, the flow is often fragmented. In many cases, the user chats with the business inside WhatsApp but has to leave the app to complete the payment in their bank, copy Pix codes, share receipts or switch between several apps. Meta was evaluating a new experience, Pix Native, designed to let the entire payment flow happen directly inside WhatsApp.

The study also sought to understand how consumers interact with business accounts run by artificial intelligence (Biz AI): whether users realize they are talking to an AI, whether they trust the answers they get, and how far they can complete their purchase journeys efficiently and satisfactorily.

More than a usability evaluation, this project had a strong focus on trust: understanding what increases or reduces users’ trust when talking to AI agents and when connecting their bank accounts to WhatsApp.

This includes perceptions of security, privacy, scams, authentication and money movement.

Objectives

Biz AI

  • Understand how consumers interact with AI-run business accounts inside WhatsApp.
  • Identify friction points across the discovery, product selection, purchase and support journey.
  • Evaluate users’ perception of the quality and intelligence of the AI agents’ answers.
  • Understand how failures, inadequate answers or handoffs to human support affect trust in the experience.

Pix Native

  • Evaluate users’ understanding of the proposal to connect a bank account to WhatsApp.
  • Explore barriers, concerns and factors that affect users’ willingness to use the feature.
  • Evaluate the onboarding and bank connection experience.
  • Evaluate the perception of security during the authentication flow.
  • Understand the acceptance of native payments made entirely inside WhatsApp.
  • Evaluate future intent to use it for everyday purchases, higher amounts and recurring payments.

Participants used:

  • their own phone;
  • their personal WhatsApp;
  • real bank accounts;
  • real money for the Pix transactions (reimbursed by Ipsos).

The Meta team watched the sessions live from the Ipsos observation room, with simultaneous Portuguese-English translation throughout fieldwork. The moderator could receive additional questions during the interviews.

Our role

At Ipsos, we owned the project end to end:

  1. Recruitment of participants.
  2. Moderation of the sessions, with the Meta team observing live.
  3. Report with the key recommendations for the design flow and the experience.

Impact

Due to confidentiality, we can’t disclose the direct results of the research. But the impact was huge:

The feature was rolled out to 60% of WhatsApp users in Brazil, with strong adoption.
