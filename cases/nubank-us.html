Nubank in the US: a foundational study.

A foundational qualitative study to understand how expats and mass affluent consumers in the United States perceive Nubank and evaluate their current financial providers.

Context

Nubank needed a foundational qualitative study to understand how its target audience in the United States, expats and mass affluent consumers, perceives the brand (likely with low or no awareness) and how they perceive, use and evaluate their current financial providers.

The goal was to build the baseline emotional and functional understanding of this audience before the brand’s next steps in the US market.

Questions

The study set out to answer three big questions:

  1. What do people think and feel about Nubank? Awareness, sentiment, instinctive impressions, credibility signals and emotional reactions.
  2. What do they think and feel about other financial brands? Emotional tone, trust levels, strengths, weak spots, unmet needs and white spaces.
  3. What opportunities exist for the brand’s narrative, positioning and value proposition as it enters the US market?

Themes explored

  • Competitor perception and usage: which providers they use, why they chose them, what each is “good for” and where they fail.
  • Nubank perception: brand awareness and first impressions of the name, visuals, story and value proposition.
  • Financial life and needs: pains, frictions, stressful moments and what “feeling in control” looks like.
  • Motivators and trust builders: what creates trust or skepticism, deal-breakers and signals of legitimacy.
  • Jobs to be done: functional, emotional and social, with specific needs for expats and for the mass affluent.
  • Mental models: how Americans see traditional banks, fintechs, global accounts and foreign financial brands.

Two audiences

  • Expats: international grad students, young professionals and working-class residents living in the US.
  • Mass affluent: US-based consumers seeking simplicity, transparency and design excellence, mostly digital natives dissatisfied with traditional banks.
  • All using several financial apps, including at least one traditional bank.

Our role

At Ipsos, we were responsible for recruitment, moderation and reporting. Deliverables included the screener, moderation guide, recruitment, videos of all sessions, daily debriefs, weekly sessions with the client’s US team, a full report and an executive summary with insights and recommendations.

I led the Ipsos US team from Brazil, coordinating the project, giving feedback on the report and making the overall research and design recommendations.
